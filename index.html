Research interests

The goal of Machine Learning Theory is to develop learning algorithms, and to understand the limitations of what can or cannot be learned. It is a vast body of research. Specifically, my research involves connections to probability theory, game theory, statistics, complexity and privacy.

Particularly, I have focused on the following research threads:

Learning in Games

Game Theory models scenarios where multiple agents interact in the same environment and influence each other. Particularly relevant is the connection to learning theory, which raised the following question: how can an agent learn in an environment with other agents, in a way that his/her learned insights are valid and meaningful, even in the presence of other agents that may have conflicting incentives?

Learning from Dependent Observations

How can we learn from data that may have significant dependencies, such as data coming from a social network, from interacting particles or from data with spatial dependencies?

Differential Privacy

How can we learn from sensitive data, such as medical or other private data, without sacrificing the individuals whose private data is used for training machine learning models?

Learning Algorithms and their Computational Limitations

What is an efficient algorithm for a given learning task, and what resources must every algorithm consume in order to succeed in a machine learning task?

Online Learning and Decision Making

How can a learning agent learn and take actions in an environment that may adapt to its actions?
